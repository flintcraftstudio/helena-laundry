## Site Structure

A tight five-page site. Everything points at one action: book a pickup. Primary CTA everywhere is call/text (406-471-8508); a question/feedback form and an out-of-area waitlist are secondary capture paths.

1. **Home** — Hero → How it works (3 steps) → What it costs (snapshot) → Some things can't be outsourced → For businesses (teaser) → Waitlist band → Ready when you are.
2. **Services & Pricing** — Full standalone price reference. Good for local SEO and direct linking.
3. **About** — Chanté's story, the "why laundry," name + sign-off. The trust anchor.
4. **For Businesses** — Expanded B2B page: standing pickups, who it's for, bulk flat rates, reliability pitch.
5. **Contact / Schedule** — Booking info (call/text), question/feedback form, out-of-area waitlist.

**Footer (all pages):** service area line, phone, no blog/FAQ at launch (fold reassurance into Services & Pricing).

**Handoff notes for the build:**
- The question form and the waitlist form have **deliberately different fields** — do not merge them into one generic contact form. The waitlist's value is clean, sortable location data.
- Anchor links `#pricing` and `#waitlist` assume pricing detail lives on the Services page and the waitlist on the Contact page. Adjust targets if placement changes.
- Capture waitlist **location** and **type** (residential vs. business) cleanly so demand is sortable later for expansion decisions.
- The public phone number will attract some spam/robocalls — worth screening unknown numbers or using a dedicated business line if it gets bad.

## How it works

Three steps. That's the whole thing.

**1. Set it out.**
Bag up your laundry and leave it on the porch at your pickup time. No need to be home. No need to sort it — that's my job.

I pick it up, bring it to my place, and sort, wash, dry, fold, and hang what needs hanging. Your clothes get their own load — never mixed in with anyone else's.

**3. Back where it started.**
Clean, folded, and organized in clear bags on your porch, ready to put away. Most orders are back in 24 to 48 hours.

# 2. Services & Pricing

**What I do, and what it costs.**

No packages to decode, no surprise fees. Here's all of it, plain.

---

**Wash, dry, fold & hang — $2.00/lb**

The everyday service. You bag it up, I pick it up, and it comes back clean, dry, folded, and hung where hanging matters. I sort it myself — whites, colors, the delicate stuff — and your laundry gets its own load. It never goes in with anyone else's.

For reference, a stuffed 13-gallon trash bag runs about $30. I won't know your exact number until it's on the scale, and I'll tell you the moment I do.

**Minimum order is $35** — about one full trash bag.

---

**Rush service — $3.00/lb**

Need it back in 24 hours or less? I'll make it happen. Same care, faster turnaround.

---

**The bulky stuff — flat rates**

Some things don't make sense by the pound, so they've got their own price:

- Twin bedding — $15
- Full bedding — $20
- Queen bedding — $30
- King bedding — $35
- Sneaker deep clean (cleaning and stain treatment) — $20
- A 13-gallon bag of towels, rags, or similar — $35

Sheet-only orders still need to hit the $35 minimum — easy enough to do with a couple of sets, or by adding a regular load on top.

---

**Stain treatment**

I'll treat what I can, and the first five items are free. After that it's $5 for six to ten items, $10 for eleven to twenty. I'll never charge you for something I couldn't get out.

---

**One thing I don't do: dry cleaning.**

I'm wash-and-fold, start to finish. If you've got dry-clean-only pieces, you'll want a dedicated cleaner for those — everything else, bring it on.

---

**Turnaround**

Most orders are back on your porch in 24 to 48 hours. Rush orders, 24 or less.

---

**Where I go**

The Helena Valley, for now. Outside it and want in? [Put your name on the list](#waitlist) — I expand toward wherever the demand is.

---

If you like how the first one goes, let's set a standing day — weekly or every other week. Same $2 a pound, and I'll bring you proper laundry bags so you can quit raiding the kitchen trash box. You can still schedule one-off pickups whenever, too.

# 3. About

**Hi, I'm Chanté.**

I grew up in Kalispell, which means I grew up knowing what Montana is worth. Then I married an Air Force man, and for a good while I saw the rest of the country whether I meant to or not — base to base, town to town. It was an education. But when the kids came along, my husband and I made a decision on purpose: we were going to raise them as Montanans. So we settled in Helena, and we've been home ever since.

I spent years working around animals and the people who love them — first as a veterinary technician, then managing a busy practice. That work teaches you how to run a real operation: keep the books straight, keep your word on the timeline, and never make a person guess what something costs.

So why laundry? Because it never stops. Everybody needs clean clothes — the overworked mom with three kids and a full-time job, the small shop going through towels by the armful — but not everybody has the time or the convenience to keep up. That's a real need, and Helena could use the help — the kind of help that has to come from someone actually here, not an app or a company three states off. I'd rather build something honest around a thing people actually need than chase something flashier. Laundry it is.

When I'm not at the machines, you'll usually find me on a horse, with my nose in a book, or working with the foster community here in town. Kids deserve a soft place to land, and Helena's good at providing it.

So here's my promise, plain as I can put it. I'll treat your laundry like it's my own family's. I'll tell you the price before you pay it. And I'll have it back on your porch when I said I would — washed, folded, and done right.

That's the whole business. I think it's enough.

**— Chanté**
